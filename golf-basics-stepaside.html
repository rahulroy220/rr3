The Fundamentals
Golf Basics

Golf is a game where players use clubs to hit a ball into a series of holes in as few strokes as possible. Here's what every beginner needs to know.

🏌️ The Object of the Game

Complete each hole by getting the ball from the tee into the hole (cup) using as few strokes as possible. A standard round is 18 holes.

  • Each hole has a designated Par (target score)
  • Lowest total score wins
  • Play alone or in groups of 2–4
  • 9-hole rounds are great for beginners
🏑 The Clubs

You can carry up to 14 clubs. Each type is designed for a different shot distance or situation.

  • Driver — longest distance, used off the tee
  • Irons (3–9) — mid-range approach shots
  • Wedges — short game, bunkers, chipping
  • Putter — used on the green to roll into the hole
  • Hybrids — easier alternatives to long irons
🗺️ The Course Layout

Each hole is made up of distinct zones you must navigate from start to finish.

  • Tee box — where you start each hole
  • Fairway — mown grass leading to the green
  • Rough — longer grass either side of the fairway
  • Green — short grass surrounding the hole/flag
  • Hazards — bunkers (sand) and water obstacles
📏 Key Rules

Golf has a formal rulebook but these basics cover most situations for new players.

  • Play the ball as it lies — don't move it
  • Out of bounds (white stakes) = 1 stroke penalty + re-hit
  • Lost ball = 1 stroke penalty + re-hit from original spot
  • In a bunker, don't touch the sand before your swing
  • On the green, don't putt while another player's ball is in the way
🤝 Etiquette

Golf has a strong culture of etiquette — following these unwritten rules is just as important as knowing the rules.

  • Stay quiet when others are taking their shot
  • Keep pace with the group ahead — don't slow down play
  • Repair divots on the fairway and pitch marks on the green
  • Rake bunkers after playing from them
  • Mobile phones on silent on the course
🎯 Handicap System

The handicap system levels the playing field so golfers of different abilities can compete fairly.

  • Beginners start with a high handicap (e.g. 28 for men, 36 for ladies)
  • Your handicap is subtracted from your gross score
  • As you improve, your handicap goes down
  • World Handicap System (WHS) is used globally
  • Get an official handicap by joining a club

Each hole has a Par — the expected number of strokes for a scratch golfer. Your score relative to Par tells you how well you played.

Getting Started
Tips for Beginners

Golf has a steep learning curve but these tips will help you enjoy the game from day one.

1
Take a few lessons first
Even 2–3 lessons with a PGA pro will set you up with a solid grip and stance, saving months of bad habits.
2
Start with a half set of clubs
You don't need 14 clubs to start. A driver, a 5-iron, a 7-iron, a wedge, and a putter is plenty for beginners.
3
Practice at a driving range first
Get comfortable hitting the ball consistently before going on a full course. Most clubs near Stepaside have practice facilities.
4
Don't keep score early on
Focus on enjoying the round and learning the course. Worrying about your score before you're ready adds unnecessary pressure.
5
Play ready golf
Whoever is ready plays next (rather than strictly farthest from the hole). This keeps pace of play up and is much friendlier for beginners.
6
Join a club for your handicap
An official handicap makes the game more fun and opens up competitions and visitor rounds. Most clubs welcome beginners warmly.
